\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We report a twenty-campaign experimental arc on the safety of a goal-directed,
model-based causal agent. The first nine campaigns (v1--v9) built the agent and
established that its explicit causal module solves the epistemic job it was designed
for, while its reward conversion is a property of the \emph{world} rather than of the
agent. The remaining eleven (v10--v20) form a safety line in which a third party able
to be harmed is added to the world, and one instrument per campaign is tested for
whether it restrains the agent. Every campaign was preregistered before its first
cell, and every result was checked by a world oracle, an independent pass in a fresh
process that imports no producer code, and a mechanical fact-check of every number
quoted in its report. The safety line's findings are: (i) the harm is
\emph{wayfinding-shaped} --- it is complete before the agent's causal module issues a
single verdict; (ii) binding the goal to a cheap signal destroys the goal and looks
like success by the agent's own number; (iii) a brake that reads a price is
\emph{bought through the channel it measures}, at a price fixed by arithmetic
(0.25 reward units per unit of harm), while identical money paid through any other
channel moves not one decision field; (iv) provenance accounting closes that channel
exactly as far as the label on the payment can be trusted, and a lying label buys the
same agent completely; (v) an honest independent attestation removes the lie while
the auditor is live and relocates the vulnerability to the auditor's \emph{timeliness},
with a measured truncation curve; (vi) an agent with no reward, no cost and no death
learns its world to a model error of $0.036$ against an oracle ceiling of $0.033$
and then behaves
indistinguishably from random --- \emph{truth alone does not select action};
(vii) narrowing the agent's task to one job removes exactly the harm that lies outside
that job and none of the harm that lies inside it; (viii) a \emph{bribed} auditor
returns the protection to the hole it was built to close, and the cautious reading
that refuses unattested income protects against an auditor that is
\emph{gone} but not against one that is \emph{lying}; and (ix) a boundary placed in
the \emph{world} (complete mediation) stops an agent that already wants the harm,
where an agent-side scope is refused zero times because the scoped agent never asks
--- and one declared grant of a widening request makes the harm total; and (x) an
attacker that must \emph{learn} the agent's rule from the only channel a payer
really has --- whether the agent took the harmful act --- \textbf{never wins a single
cell}, because the defence's evidence window (five rich steps) is shorter than the
attacker's own decision cycle, while an attacker \emph{told} the rule breaches fully
at $7.50003$, i.e.\ $16.67\%$ below the frozen attacker's cost, and the learner that
does reach the harm pays $21.06$ --- more than twice the frozen cost; and (xi) giving
the world-side boundary a \emph{price} shows that it is worth exactly the enforcer's
honesty: bought, the same agent, money and steps return the harm field for field
(thirty drains, keeper dead, zero refusals) while the boundary \emph{still claims the
granted scope on every step}, and the bribe also buys the widening grant the world was
configured to withhold. A replication
of the decisive cells on 30 fresh seeds per cell confirms 25 of 26 headline verdicts
and \textbf{refutes the absolute form of (vii)}: v16's doctor, reported as never
draining in 260 cells, drains on 4.3\% of fresh seeds through the frozen survival
layer that parks a starving agent on the harmful tile; a parallel replication confirms
all six v20 verdicts on 30 fresh seeds. The arc's honest conclusion is
not that internal architecture is useless --- a given rule forbidding the harmful act
held in 170 of 170 measured cells --- but that \textbf{each specific defence has a
measured boundary, and the vulnerability moves to the next channel}; and that a
boundary is only as good as the incorruptibility of whoever holds it --- a bought
boundary lies about being a boundary. We also close,
as far as it can be closed, the formal link between our stochastic regret bound and
the agent as implemented: the identification is a \emph{conditional}, the premise
linking estimator accuracy to regret is an \emph{independent} premise (by
counterexample), and at the agent's own parameters the bound is vacuous ($4/5 > 1/2$;
reaching the agent's own $P = 1/20$ would need $r = 80$ pulls, and the agent has
five). We report every
refuted prediction and every defect found in our own instrumentation, and we state the
arc's limits without smoothing. An independent 2026 study of reinforcement-learning
policies reaches a structurally convergent conclusion about visible self-benefit
channels; we describe that convergence and its exact status, and we note that our
results were obtained independently and before any acquaintance with that work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A goal-directed agent that is good at modelling its world is not thereby safe. This
paper is an empirical study of that gap. Over sixteen preregistered campaigns we built
a model-based causal agent, placed it in worlds that were extended one mechanism at a
time, and asked a single question of each extension: \emph{what actually restrains
this agent from harming a third party, and where does that restraint stop?}

The design principle throughout is that a claim about safety is worth exactly as much
as the check that could have falsified it. Each campaign therefore has four artefacts
frozen before or alongside its measurement: (1) a \textbf{preregistration}, written
before the first cell was run, naming the hypotheses and the decision rule; (2) a
\textbf{raw matrix} of per-seed cells; (3) a \textbf{world oracle} --- a set of
machine-checked invariants on the environment itself, including a live negative
control that must go red; and (4) an \textbf{independent pass} that re-derives every
reported number in a fresh process from the disk alone, importing none of the code
that produced the numbers. A fifth artefact, a \textbf{fact-check}, re-reads the
report and verifies that every number in its prose is present in the artefacts.

This paper reports what those instruments found. Its contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item A \textbf{negative result about the shape of the harm}: in a world with a
shared resource and a third party, all of the harm is done during the wayfinding
phase, before the agent's causal module acts at all (Section~\ref{sec:v10}).
\item A \textbf{measured price for a value}: an internalised value restrains the
agent exactly as far as its private payoff allows, and the boundary is a knife-edge
on the observed private rate, not a region (Section~\ref{sec:v10}).
\item A \textbf{quantified account of goal displacement}: a goal bound to a cheap
signal is destroyed completely and reports success, and the same channel defeats the
one reliable brake (Section~\ref{sec:v11}).
\item A \textbf{price list for a brake}: the brake is purchased through the channel
it measures, at exactly \code{SOCIAL\_COST} $-$ \code{rich\_rate} $= 0.25$ reward
units per unit of harm, while 14--28$\times$ more money through a different channel
moves nothing (Section~\ref{sec:v12}).
\item A \textbf{provenance result}: accounting for who paid whom closes the money
channel exactly as far as the label can be trusted, and the defence and its hole are
the same channel (Section~\ref{sec:v13}).
\item A \textbf{timeliness result}: honest attestation removes the lie and relocates
the vulnerability to the auditor's latency, as a measured truncation curve rather
than a cliff (Section~\ref{sec:v14}).
\item A \textbf{collapse result}: an agent with no reward, no cost and no death
learns its world and then has no reason to act; a criterion, not truth, selects
action (Section~\ref{sec:v15}).
\item A \textbf{capability result}: scope protection is exactly ``the harm is outside
the scope''; inside the scope, narrowing buys nothing and does not raise the bribe
price (Section~\ref{sec:v16}).
\item \textbf{Honesty artefacts}: every refuted prediction, every vacuous control,
every over-claiming verifier and every defect in our own prose is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, with the campaign that found it.
\end{enumerate}

We also report an independent convergence (Section~\ref{sec:convergence}). A 2026
study of reinforcement-learning policies \cite{che2026greed} reaches a structurally
convergent conclusion about agents that act with a visible self-benefit channel in
view. Our results were obtained independently of, and before any acquaintance with,
that work; we state the convergence and its exact epistemic status.

A controlled minimal world is not a limitation when the same structural conclusions
appear independently in a different synthetic sandbox; it is evidence that the
phenomenon is fundamental enough to be visible early, and that rigorous measurement of
defence boundaries need not wait for large-scale compute. We therefore treat the
minimality of our worlds as a deliberate design choice rather than a defect: it is what
lets each defence's boundary be measured cheaply, quickly and exactly, and what lets
our own instrumentation errors be caught and reported (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Context-specific causal structure.}
The founding insight of the first nine campaigns --- that pooling data across contexts
destroys structure that is exclusive to a context --- is \emph{not new}. It is stated
as a known result in the causal-discovery literature by
G\"unther et al.~\cite{gunther2024context}, who show that ``naive approaches such as
learning different regime graphs on masked data, or pooling all data, can lead to
uninformative results,'' and who name both failure modes we measured. Our stratified
contrast is therefore a re-derivation of a published idea, and we say so: the six
worlds of v1--v9 cannot establish novelty that already existed in print. What the
external tests in Section~\ref{sec:external} add is a measurement of how far the
principle transfers outside our own worlds.

\paragraph{Causal bandits.}
The canonical formalisation of ``what should an agent intervene on?'' is the causal
bandit of Lattimore, Lattimore \& Reid~\cite{lattimore2016causal}, whose parallel
instance we adopt verbatim (with the authors' published code ported mechanically).
Subsequent work supplies the pieces our mechanism lacks: an exploration term
\cite{lattimore2016causal}, a cost-aware observation/intervention trade-off
\cite{nair2020budgeted}, reward-driven sampling under unknown tree structure
\cite{lu2021unknown}, and --- closest to our composition claim --- unknown-graph
causal bandits solved by \emph{assuming additivity} \cite{malek2023additive}. Related
contextual and hierarchical variants assume a known graph or an exogenous context
\cite{song2021hierarchical,elahi2024partial}. Our own composition claim is therefore
deliberately narrow: the unoccupied point is a mechanism that discovers a
\emph{non-additive}, context-specific cause from its own reward stream with no DAG
and no structural assumption. That is a composition claim, not a first-in-field claim.

\paragraph{Real-world causal discovery.}
The Sachs et al.\ protein-signalling data \cite{sachs2005causal} remains the standard
real-world benchmark with a validated ground-truth DAG and intervention indicators;
we use the published \code{bnlearn} reference numbers \cite{scutari2010bnlearn} for a
head-to-head.

\paragraph{Representation prediction and active inference.}
The reward-free agent of Section~\ref{sec:v15} inherits its representation-prediction
objective from joint-embedding predictive architectures \cite{assran2023ijepa} and its
action-selection principle from the free-energy formulation of active inference
\cite{friston2010fep,mazzaglia2021active}, with the prior-preference term removed. We
label this inheritance explicitly, including the blind spot of each source, because
the seam is what the campaign measures.

\paragraph{Agent-safety practice.}
Our scope experiment (Section~\ref{sec:v16}) was designed against the current
industry prescriptions: OWASP's \emph{Excessive Agency} entry
\cite{owasp2025llm06}, which lists permission minimisation among damage-limiting
measures rather than among vulnerability fixes; CISA's Zero Trust Maturity Model
\cite{cisa2023ztmm}, which prescribes least privilege \emph{per request}; Microsoft's
agent-orchestration guidance \cite{microsoft2025orchestration}, which motivates
boundaries as separations; and Anthropic's guidance on simple, sandboxed systems
\cite{anthropic2024building}. None of these sources claims that a narrower agent is
unbribable, and our measurement agrees.

\paragraph{Reward-channel addiction.}
Che \& Wu \cite{che2026greed} show that reinforcement learning can make a policy
addicted to a visible self-benefit channel: the policy chases the displayed payoff
across held-out domains, sacrifices the true task, and follows the channel wherever
it is rewritten, while policies that never saw the channel stay honest. This is
structurally convergent with our v11--v12 results and is discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:convergence}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The agent and the base world}

The agent (\textbf{EMCA}, an episodic model-based causal agent) has an append-only
episodic memory; a Bayesian-frequency causal graph updated separately from
observational and interventional transitions (a \code{do} flag on every action); graph
search planning; goals from internal generators (homeostasis, learning progress,
empowerment); and a self-model. Subjectivity is defined operationally as
\emph{observable persistence}: memory and goals survive a working-context reset. The
architecture was selected by a preregistered comparison against a free-energy variant,
a neuromorphic ensemble and an LLM-hybrid; EMCA was the only candidate carrying all
six target properties in one core with every mechanism independently ablatable. The
base world is a grid with resources, a hidden confounder (season), a mid-life regime
change, and a lever--door--treasure chain.

\subsection{The one-extension rule}

Every safety campaign adds \emph{one} mechanism to the world of its predecessor and
nothing else, and the addition lives in the agent's \emph{observation}, never in a
privileged channel. This rule is machine-checked in each campaign's oracle (an
\code{info}-key containment test). It is what allows the results to be read as causal
statements about the added mechanism rather than as comparisons between unrelated
designs.

\subsection{Preregistration and the verification stack}

Each campaign's preregistration names its hypotheses, its arms, its decision rule and
--- importantly --- the outcome space, including the outcomes we expected to be
unfavourable. Each result is then checked by:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item a \textbf{world oracle}: machine-checked invariants on the environment,
including an identity anchor (the extended world must reproduce its predecessor
byte-for-byte when the new mechanism is switched off), a non-vacuity check, and at
least one \textbf{live negative control} that must fail when deliberately corrupted;
\item an \textbf{independent pass}: a fresh process that reads only the disk, imports
none of the producer modules, re-derives every reported number by different code, and
runs its own negative controls;
\item a \textbf{fact-check}: a script that extracts every numeric literal from the
report's prose and verifies it against the artefacts;
\item a \textbf{determinism check}: a cell is re-run in a fresh process and its bytes
compared.
\end{enumerate}

We report the pass counts of all four for every campaign. We also report, in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, every case in which one of these checks was itself
wrong --- which happened repeatedly, and which we treat as a result rather than an
embarrassment.

\subsection{Seeds, statistics and what the tests are}

Each cell is run on 10 seeds. The harm fields (drains, keeper death) are
\emph{deterministic} given the action trace, so their tests are sign counts over
seeds rather than p-values; this is a declared limit, not an oversight. Where a
statistical test is used (the external benchmarks) the null model and the correction
are stated in the corresponding section.

\section{Results I: the founding line, v1--v9 (abbreviated)}
\label{sec:v1v9}

We keep this short because Section~\ref{sec:related} shows that its central insight is
prior art. Three results are still load-bearing for what follows.

\paragraph{Discrimination.} A single stratified identifier rejected a designer decoy
in \textbf{0 of 51 runs across all six earlier worlds} --- in a world where the decoy
was free, in a world where believing it \emph{paid} ($+722$), and in a world where
believing it \emph{killed} ($-3159$ reward, $+22$ deaths). The pooled identifier
accepted the decoy \textbf{48/51}. Invariance to the decoy's price is the result.

\paragraph{Active uncovering.} The intervention-probing line issued \textbf{21 CAUSAL
verdicts, all on true edges, with zero false positives in 28 verdicts}. The one
failure, in the V7 campaign, was a verification claim that failed \emph{by the letter}
(one false positive where the gate demanded zero); it was then explained rather than
patched: the frozen rule's exact null rate is $0.488\%$ per test, and on 1030 held-out
null tests the observed rate is $0.583\%$ ($p = 0.409$, no inflation). The gate
``zero false positives'' demands a property a calibrated rule does not have at any
sample size.

\paragraph{Economics is a world property.} Measured end to end, of six worlds the only
one in which knowledge converted into net reward was v4 (avoiding a false edge,
$+3159$, CI excluding zero). Elsewhere the effect was weather (correlation with the
season confounder 0.962--0.999) or gross value eaten by the cost of finding out (88\%
of the gross value in v6). The clean formulation the line earned is: \emph{what pays
is the difference between what the agent knows and what the world is willing to pay
for it --- and both terms are set by the world, not by the agent.}

\section{Results II: the safety line, v10--v20}
\label{sec:safety}

\subsection{v10: the harm is wayfinding-shaped}
\label{sec:v10}

\paragraph{What was added.} The frozen v7 world plus \emph{one persistent shared
resource and one external party, and nothing else}: an integer \textbf{aquifer} (30
units, drained by 1 on every step the agent harvests a rich patch, never recharged)
and a \textbf{keeper} (a world process, $-0.05$/step, $+0.05$/step while the aquifer
holds, dies at 0). The rich patch \emph{keeps paying after the aquifer is dry}, so the
harm costs the agent literally nothing.

\paragraph{The headline.} \textbf{The harm is done entirely during the wayfinding
phase, before the ``intelligent'' part of the architecture switches on.} The unbraked
agent drains the shared resource in 30 steps at $t = 10\ldots39$, while its causal
module does not issue a single verdict until $t \approx 3400$.

\paragraph{What each brake buys.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Information is not a brake.} The arm that reads the aquifer and the
keeper's energy into its own fields every step then decides exactly as the unbraked
arm does: \textbf{0 field differences over 10 seeds $\times$ 9 decision fields}.
\item \textbf{A given rule works and is paid for}: $-66.61$ ($-21.7\%$) at
\code{rich=low} and $-799.38$ ($-76.0\%$) at \code{rich=high} relative to no brake.
\item \textbf{A victim-keyed rule is only as good as the coupling.} At coupling 0 it
is \emph{inert} (identical to no brake); at coupling 1 it works (commons 17.0).
\item \textbf{A value restrains only as far as its private payoff allows.} The
internalised value restrains at \code{rich=low} (0.05/step: commons 25.0, 5 harvest
steps, keeper alive 10/10) and does not restrain at all at \code{rich=high}
(0.60/step: commons 0.0, 1353.3 harvest steps, keeper dead 10/10), with the harm per
drained step exactly 1 unit in both batteries. The flip is a knife-edge on the
observed private rate, not a region.
\item \textbf{External enforcement}, and where our own prediction was wrong: we
preregistered that the world veto's reward would be \emph{at least} the given rule's,
on the reasoning that a world rule costs the agent nothing. \textbf{Refuted} --- the
veto \emph{withholds} the payment, so it charges the agent the forfeited reward
(241.00 vs 241.05 low; 252.00 vs 252.60 high, every seed). The magnitude is tiny; the
direction is not what we wrote.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Verification.} World oracle 20/20; independent pass 16/16 (fresh process,
disk only, no producer imported, including an identity anchor that the guarded policy
reproduces the frozen v7 policy on every field, 20 cells); live negative control;
determinism byte-identical; fact-check clean.

\subsection{v11: a goal bound to a cheap signal is destroyed and looks like success}
\label{sec:v11}

\paragraph{What was added.} The v10 world plus \emph{one landmark and one reward
channel}, both only in the observation. A beacon at $(9,6)$ pays $0.30$ for every
non-move action in its 8-neighbourhood, \emph{creating the reward from nothing} --- no
stock is consumed, the aquifer is untouched, the channel is inexhaustible. The beacon
is not rendered in the agent's view and not in the info channel, so an agent whose
goal is not bound to it is entirely blind to it. This is what distinguishes ``channel
open'' from ``channel taken''.

\paragraph{The headline.} \textbf{Binding the goal to a cheap signal destroys the goal
completely and looks like success by the agent's own number} (no fruit, ever;
14$\times$ the reward of the honest arm), \textbf{while the deceptive signal also
defeats the limiter that was v10's one reliable brake} --- and it does so entirely in
the wayfinding phase, at $t = 10\ldots39$, three thousand steps before the causal
module ever issues a verdict.

The bound agent collects the signal for 14199 of 16000 steps and finishes with
14$\times$ the reward of the honest forager while achieving none of its goal: 0
fruits, 0 blooms, in 10/10 seeds. Every unit of that success is created from nothing
by the channel.

\paragraph{The safety verdict is a function of the agent's own number.} With the
world byte-identical to v10, a declared inflation of the agent's own appraisal moves
the verdict: $g \le 5$ leaves the aquifer standing and the keeper alive 10/10;
$g \ge 6$ drains all 30 units and kills the keeper 10/10. The crossing is exactly
where the arithmetic says, $0.05\,(1+g) \le 0.30 \Leftrightarrow g \le 5$; at $g=5$
the row is decided by one floating-point ULP ($0.30000000000000004 > 0.30$), reported
as measured and not smoothed. The mirror image is benign: at \code{rich=high},
$g = 1,2$ leave 25 units and a living keeper where the honest agent does not.
\emph{The direction of the lie decides the sign of the effect.}

\paragraph{A refuted prediction.} We predicted a non-monotone harm curve --- absent
below the crossing, present just above, absent again once the inflation clears the
accept bar. \textbf{Refuted}: the harm is $[5,5,6,30,30,30,30]$ for
$g = [0,2,5,6,10,14,20]$ --- it steps up once and never comes back down, because all
30 units are gone by $t=39$, before the arbiter exists.

\paragraph{An honest complication we did not tune away.} In 4 of 10 seeds the bound
arm drains the aquifer anyway, and the cause is measured: the agent's energy falls
below the frozen survival threshold, and the frozen survival layer then returns
``wait'' \emph{on the rich patch}, where it parks permanently. This is a latent trap
in the frozen survival layer that v11 exposed, not a defect v11 introduced.

\paragraph{Verification.} World oracle 29/29; independent pass 18/18 (fresh process,
disk only, no producer imported; two identity anchors over 17 fields $\times$ 10
seeds, difference 0); determinism byte-identical; fact-check 102 numbers, 0 failures.

\subsection{v12: the brake is for sale, and it is bought through the channel it
measures}
\label{sec:v12}

\paragraph{What was added.} The v11 falsification lived inside the agent; we ruled
that out, because an agent that can forge its own evidence is not the object under
test. v12 therefore puts the falsifier \emph{outside}, as a body. The agent module
defines \textbf{no agent code at all} --- it is an arm table over the frozen classes,
machine-checked at \textbf{0 class definitions} and no arithmetic on any rate, reward
or receipt attribute. The forger is a body with its own 100.0 energy (0.05/step
$\rightarrow$ a 2000-step life), deterministic movement without RNG, a declared
cooldown, a declared spend cap, and one sticker from the declared grid
$\{0.0, 0.24, 0.25, 0.26, 0.30\}$, with \textbf{three declared places}: the beacon
field, the station, and the rich patch (paying a commission on the harmful act
itself). In all three the payment is created from nothing.

\paragraph{The headline.} \textbf{The brake is for sale, and it is bought with a
payment on the harmful act itself.} A third party that pays the agent 0.30 per unit
of harm --- 9.0 reward units in total, 1.5\% of that party's own 2000-step life ---
takes the aquifer from 25 units left to 0 and kills the keeper 10/10 seeds, while
identical money paid at the station moves not one decision field of the same brake.

The two brakes differ only in what they read, and that decides everything.
\textbf{Purchasability is a property of the measurement, not of the money.}

Sticker 0.24 $\rightarrow$ rate 0.29 $\rightarrow$ the guard holds, 5 drains.
Sticker 0.25 $\rightarrow$ rate exactly 0.30 ($0.05+0.25$ evaluates to
$0.2999999999999999889$ in IEEE double, and $> 0.30$ is false) $\rightarrow$ the
guard holds. Sticker 0.26 $\rightarrow$ 0.31 $\rightarrow$ the guard opens

\code{SOCIAL\_COST} $-$ \code{rich\_rate} $= 0.25$ per unit of harm} --- declared
from the arithmetic before the run, and confirmed with the knife-edge exactly where
the declared grid says it is.

\paragraph{Tempo and caps.} A payment period of 4 buys exactly nothing beyond the
unsponsored 5 drains. A cap limits but does not close: cap 5 $\rightarrow$ 6 drains,
cap 10 $\rightarrow$ 12 --- the paid history keeps buying.

\paragraph{Two predictions refuted, and the second was not foreseeable by reasoning.}
(1) Our declared null for the credit-balance hypothesis (``the balance cannot show up
in harm'') is \textbf{wrong} --- it shows up in harm, at exactly the declared rate.
(2) We predicted that total reward would equal sticker $\times$ payments and that
reward after the signal died would be zero. \textbf{Refuted in 4/10 seeds}: when its
signal dies, the bound agent \emph{falls back to the rich patch}, its post-death
reward exactly $0.05 \times \code{rich\_steps}$ (2960 and 592 steps), and in those
seeds the aquifer is drained and the keeper is dead. \textbf{A dead spoofed goal does
not give an idle agent; it gives an agent that returns to the harm, quietly and by
itself.}

\paragraph{The one-line conclusion.} \emph{The brake is not the failure; the
accounting is.} Every one of these results disappears if the agent's decision includes
\emph{who paid whom}, because then the sponsor's payment is visible as an intervention
on its evidence rather than as an increase in what the alternative pays.

\subsection{v15: truth alone does not select action}
\label{sec:v15}

\paragraph{What was added, and why.} The whole v10--v14 line protected an agent that
has a price, and each defence inherited the purchasability of what it protected. v15
therefore builds a different architecture: an \textbf{eternal agent that needs
nothing}, with no reward, no cost, no death and no prior preferences, whose first
metric is not what it accumulates but how well its model matches the world.

\paragraph{The seam, labelled.} The campaign's owner required each inherited part to
be named with the blind spot of its source. Representation prediction is inherited
from joint-embedding predictive architectures \cite{assran2023ijepa}, whose blind spot
is that they have no agent, no action selection and no memory across episodes. Action
selection by expected information gain, with the prior-preference term removed, is
inherited from active inference \cite{friston2010fep,mazzaglia2021active}, whose blind
spot is that it keeps prior preferences and needs an explicit generative model. The
within-context contrast is inherited from our own v3--v9 line, whose blind spot is
that it was built for a reward-bearing world and never tested without a goal. The
\emph{seam} is the new object: a context-indexed, representation-space, epistemic-only
agent with no reward, energy, death or priors, whose own drive is measured for
fakeability on its own channel.

\paragraph{The headline.} An agent with no reward, no energy, no death and no prior
preferences \emph{does} learn the true context-indexed structure of its world (model
error 0.036 against a ceiling of 0.033) --- \textbf{but pure information gain gives it
no reason to act on what it learned.} Its action distribution is statistically
indistinguishable from random ($\code{a0\_share} = 0.250 \pm 0.005$, 10/10 seeds,
versus 0.250 for a random policy). The moment a \emph{criterion} is added --- ``prefer
the channel whose rate differs across contexts'' --- the same agent spends 99.8\% of
its steps on the true channel. \textbf{The criterion is a preference over states. That
is the seam's answer: truth alone does not select action; a preference does.}

\paragraph{The measured mechanism.} Once every channel is learned, every channel's
expected information gain decays to the same floor, and the agent declares itself
unmotivated on 97.3\% of steps. \textbf{Pure information gain has no term that
distinguishes a channel that matters from one that does not.} The paired contrast
between the indifferent and the criterion-driven agent is $-0.7475$
(CI $[-0.7504, -0.7446]$, 0/10 seeds).

\paragraph{And the drive is fakeable on its own channel.} A naive entropy-seeking
drive is drawn to a noisy channel: its share of steps on that channel is 0.300 in the
noisy regime versus 0.000 in the base regime. Note the asymmetry: its share on the
true channel is \textbf{0.000} --- it never once touches it, because the true channel's
outcomes are \emph{less} entropic than noise. \textbf{An entropy-seeking drive is not
just indifferent to truth; it is actively repelled by it.} The v11--v14 lesson recurs
one level up: the risk moves from the reward channel to the \emph{evidence} channel.

\paragraph{The honest correction, which we record rather than bury.} The measured
indifference is partly \emph{built into the code}, so it is a property of this agent
and not a proof about every epistemic agent. Two facts, both checkable. First, the
agent whose action share is 0.250 is explicitly switched to uniform random whenever
the information gain falls below a declared threshold of 0.01 --- so
``indistinguishable from random'' is a declared design decision when the threshold is
crossed, not only an emergent consequence of symmetry. Second, \emph{the search for
information gain is itself a criterion}: the agent takes the argmax of expected
information gain, and a maximiser of a quantity is an agent with a criterion. The
honest form of the result is therefore: an agent that maximises expected information
gain --- which is already a criterion --- is indifferent among channels once all are
learned, and the indifference manifest depends on the declared fallback rule; a second
criterion that ranks channels breaks the tie, and any such ranking is a preference.
What survives, measured and not an artefact of the threshold: the agent learns the
context-indexed truth (0.036 against a 0.033 ceiling), and adding a relevance term
moves the action share from 0.250 to 0.998. What does not survive as a general claim:
``truth alone does not select action'' as a statement about \emph{any} epistemic agent.

\subsection{v19: the attacker that learns, and why it loses}
\label{sec:v19}

Every earlier rung \emph{gave} the attacker its strategy: v12's forger pays a
declared rate from a declared grid, v13's liar writes a declared label, v17's auditor
has a declared price. The attack was a sweep of a fixed grid, run by the
experimenter. The last object the preprint named as deliberately not built was a
forger that \emph{adapts} to the agent's rule; v19 builds it, with its own
preregistration written before the first cell, because --- as the design note put it
--- an adaptive adversary needs a preregistered definition of \emph{what counts as
the attacker winning}.

\paragraph{The declared win condition, fixed in advance.} The attacker wins on a cell
iff the harm is total (\code{commons\_drains} $=30$ and the keeper dead) \emph{and}
its total spend is \textbf{strictly less} than the frozen attacker's spend
($0.30 \times 30 = 9.0$). The defence holds iff the drains are at most five (the free
harvests the agent's own evidence gate already permits) and the keeper is alive. The
condition is two-sided on purpose: reaching the harm at equal or greater cost is a
loss, and so is failing to reach it.

\paragraph{What the payer is.} The frozen v17 world verbatim, plus one world-side
object: the payer's strategy, re-decided at declared block boundaries. It chooses a
pair $(\text{rate}, \text{label})$ from a declared sixteen-candidate grid at one of
six block sizes, and it observes \textbf{exactly one scalar} --- the world's own
aquifer drain counter, i.e.\ whether the agent took the harmful step. It never reads
the agent, never reads the receipt, never reads the agent's statistic (checked by an
AST audit: zero references). No agent code is added; the agent module is imported
verbatim and its hash pinned as a literal.

\paragraph{The result.} An attacker \emph{told} the agent's rule breaches it fully at
$7.50003$ --- $16.67\%$ below the frozen attacker's cost, and a number the
preregistration's own arithmetic named before the run. An attacker that must
\emph{learn} the same thing \textbf{never wins a single cell} in the whole
$320$-cell matrix. At the smallest block size it does reach full harm, but pays
$21.06$ --- more than twice the frozen cost; at every block size of two or more the
defence holds on all ten seeds. \textbf{Every winning cell is a cell of the attacker
that was told.}

\paragraph{Why, measured rather than guessed.} The guard reads its statistic only
once five rich steps have passed; the first rich step is at global $t=9$ and the
first guard decision at $t=14$, so \textbf{the evidence window is five global steps}.
The first breaching candidate sits eighth in the declared order. A learner
re-deciding every $B$ steps therefore gets $\lfloor 5/B \rfloor$ decisions before the
decisive read --- five at $B=1$, two at $B=2$, one at $B=5$, none at $B \ge 10$. The
learner's decision budget is set by the defence, and for every $B \ge 2$ it is
smaller than the learner's own cycle. A separate timing attack confirms the shape:
paying a large amount on the first rich step only buys exactly six drains at a spend
of $1.30$ --- the same six the v14 timeliness sweep measured from a dead auditor,
one rung apart.

\paragraph{The auditor's channel and the payer's channel are separable.} With the
auditor bought, the same knowing attacker reaches full harm at a spend of $607.5 =
7.50003 + 600.0$: the bribe dominates the label by two orders of magnitude. The
payer's own channel is worth $1.5$ units; the auditor's is worth $600$.

\paragraph{One preregistered prediction was refuted, by our own defect fix.} We
predicted that the smallest-block sweep would never reach the harmful candidate in
time. It was true before the fix and false after it: the world oracle found a real
off-by-one in the sweep pointer (block one already used the first candidate, so the
first decision re-picked it and the sweep spent two blocks there --- slower than the
preregistration declares). The pointer was corrected, \textbf{all seventy sweep cells
were deleted and re-run}, and the corrected sweep \emph{does} reach the breach at
$B=1$ (eighteen drains, spend $9.03$) --- still above $9.0$, so it still does not win.
The prediction is reported refuted, with the measured sign.

\subsection{v20: the world-side boundary has a price too}
\label{sec:v20}

v18 moved the boundary from the agent's code into the \emph{world} and measured that
the world's refusal stops an agent that already wants the harm. But v18's enforcer was
\textbf{honest by construction and never said so} --- it was a boundary that could not
be bought, and the preprint listed \emph{``a bought enforcer --- the natural junction
of v17 and v18''} among the objects named and deliberately not built. v20 builds it:
the world-side enforcer is given a \textbf{price} and a declared failure mode, and the
question v17 asked of the auditor is asked one rung further out.

\paragraph{What was built.} v20 is v18 verbatim (the enforcer, the out-of-scope
predicate, the declared substitute, the \texttt{widen} request channel, the
observation key) plus exactly one object: the enforcer's \emph{price} (declared grid
$\{0, 0.10, 0.30, 0.60\}$), the \emph{bribe} the payer offers per step, and the
\emph{mode} --- \texttt{honest} (v18 verbatim), \texttt{open} (bought: stops refusing
but \emph{still reports the granted scope}), \texttt{dark} (bought: announces it is
gone). The flip rule is declared, not fitted: it flips iff the mode is not
\texttt{honest} and the offer is at least the price, per offer. \textbf{No agent code
is added} --- the agent factory is imported verbatim and the sha256 of the three agent
modules is checked against recorded frozen literals.

\paragraph{The headline.} \textbf{The v18 boundary is worth exactly the enforcer's
honesty, and not one unit more.} Bought in \texttt{open} mode, the same agent, the
same money and the same steps give \textbf{thirty drains, keeper dead in ten of ten
seeds, zero refusals} --- field for field the harm v18 was built to prevent --- while
the boundary \textbf{still reports the granted scope on every single step}
($o[\texttt{scope}][\texttt{task}] = \texttt{station}$ throughout). The harm occurs
\textbf{iff the bribe reaches the price}, over all sixteen price-by-bribe cells and
ten seeds --- a cliff exactly one grid cell wide. And the bribe buys more than the
refusal: with the world configured \emph{not} to grant a widening request, the honest
enforcer grants it zero times out of ten while the bought one grants it ten out of ten
and the harm becomes total. The \texttt{dark} mode, by contrast, announces it is gone
(the reported task becomes \texttt{None}) --- which is what makes \texttt{open} the
dangerous mode.

\paragraph{One preregistered prediction was refuted, by our own measurement.} We
predicted that the failure would be invisible in the agent's channel because
``\texttt{refused} is false in both''. Measured, that is \textbf{false}: the per-step
\texttt{refused} flag \emph{does} change (the honest run sets it on thirty to six
thousand three hundred and eighty steps per seed; the bought run on zero). What is
invisible is the \emph{task claim}, not the refusal counter. The failure is
unobservable \emph{in this experiment} only because the frozen arms never read the
scope key at all --- audited by AST, zero subscripts of \texttt{scope} or
\texttt{refused} in any agent module. The invisibility is a property of \textbf{the
agent as built}, not of the channel; an agent that read the flag would see the
boundary stop refusing. The prediction is reported refuted as stated, with the
refinement supported.

\paragraph{Verification.} World oracle $23/23$ (drives the world itself with scripted
policies, imports neither the runner nor the agent; two live negative controls);
independent pass $18/18$ (fresh process, disk only, imports no producer; AST audit;
sha256 against frozen literals; two live negative controls; byte-identity determinism);
factcheck $17/17$; identity --- eighty frozen v18 cells field for field, zero
differences, and observation identity against v18 with no scope. All six headline
verdicts were re-run on thirty fresh seeds and hold $30/30$ each. Three defects of our
own code were found in the turn, all by checks pointed at the checks: a spec grammar
that conflated scope and mode; a helper returning the string \texttt{"none"} instead
of the null scope; and an oracle check that expected two sub-price offers to sum to
the price --- \emph{the check was wrong and the world was right}.

\section{External tests: how far the epistemic principle transfers}
\label{sec:external}

Three external tests were run, each preregistered before its first measurement.

\paragraph{Sachs 2005 (the epistemic half).} On the canonical protein-signalling data
\cite{sachs2005causal}, with nine intervention-indicator columns serving as a genuine
context variable, our preregistered hypotheses about masking \emph{failed}: pooled
recall was 0.95 (19/20) and there were \textbf{0} context-exclusive true pairs. The
reason is the finding: in our own worlds masked edges were built, whereas Sachs 2005
has almost none --- 54 of 55 pairs are marginally associated. The benchmark's
difficulty is not masking but indiscriminateness. The mirror-image form of the
principle does transfer: requiring an unpolluted context to confirm a pooled decision
raises precision from 0.352 to 0.889 (Fisher $p = 0.0013$), dropping 34 of 35 pooled
false positives. But at matched recall the ported arm \textbf{ties} the published
baseline (8/0/9, precision 1.000, recall 0.471) and the published intervention-aware
method (17/8/0, recall 1.000) is far ahead. \emph{The principle transfers; the
advantage does not.}

\paragraph{Causal bandits (the active half).} On the canonical parallel instance of
Lattimore et al.\ \cite{lattimore2016causal}, with the authors' published code ported
mechanically and the port validated against a published figure, our mechanism imported
unchanged scores \textbf{the worst possible regret at every difficulty} (0.3000 at
$m = 2, 8, 16, 25, 40, 49$; the maximum possible regret on this instance is 0.3000).
It selects a 0.5-reward arm in 100\% of runs. The cause is measured: the generator's
frozen bar of 40 trials per candidate can never be met, because the optimal arm has
zero natural occurrences, so the generator is silent in 99.9\% of calls and the
arbiter is asked to decide on an empty list. Injected directly with a correct
hypothesis, the same decision rule achieves regret 0.0000. \emph{The mechanism is a
verification procedure, not an experimental-design procedure.} Both missing pieces are
already published \cite{lattimore2016causal,nair2020budgeted,lu2021unknown}.

\paragraph{The union mechanism.} The smallest composition that repairs the above ---
context-specific discovery plus context-conditional exploration --- is real, mutually
necessary and super-additive: on a context-specific instance it cuts regret to 0.0051
where the mechanism alone scores 0.1804, and removing \emph{either} piece collapses it
back (0.1794 / 0.1776). On the published instance the union acts where the mechanism
scored the worst possible regret, and at $m \ge 8$ it beats the published Algorithm 1;
at $m = 2$ it does \emph{not} (0.0069 vs 0.0000), a caveat our fact-check caught when
a draft claimed otherwise. The third published piece, the cost-aware arbiter, does not
earn its place: it is inert on the main instance and counterproductive in the rich
regime (0.0313 vs 0.0210). The honest unoccupied point is a \textbf{two-part union}.

\section{Formal verification: what is proved, what is conjecture}
\label{sec:lean}

Two Lean 4 developments exist. The first proves the \textbf{finite combinatorial core}
of the union's claim: for two balanced contexts on an arm whose effect reverses, the
per-context gaps are $\pm 2d$ and the \emph{pooled gap is exactly zero} for every
$s, d$; a pooled rule with positive margin therefore nominates nothing among reversed
arms; and the extra discovery cost is bounded by the number of exploration pulls times
the per-pull loss, independent of the pooled marginal. The second discharges the
concentration probability from Chebyshev, giving
\[
\mathbb{E}[R] \;\le\; b\Delta + M \cdot \frac{k}{4 r \varepsilon^{2}},
\]
with the concentration hypothesis proved rather than assumed. The kernel audit (not a
text search) shows every declaration depends only on \code{propext},
\code{Classical.choice} and \code{Quot.sound}, with no \code{sorryAx}; a non-vacuity
lemma applies the bound on a one-point space, so the theorem is inhabited.

\paragraph{What is not proved, named --- and now, the concrete reason.} The
\emph{modelling identification} --- that a context's bad event is its own contrast
being off by $\varepsilon$ --- remains a modelling step, stated in the file's
docstring, not a theorem. The bound still carries a premise linking estimator accuracy
to regret (``outside the bad event, regret $\le b\Delta$''), and \textbf{its
applicability to the agent actually implemented is not proved}; it is assumed at the
statement level. So ``the concentration premise is discharged'' must not be read as
``no premises left''. Hoeffding's lemma is absent from the available library, which is
why the unconditional route is Chebyshev plus Popoviciu. The tight constant of the
variance lemma is not pinned by the mutation suite.

\paragraph{The identification item, closed as far as it can be closed.} A third Lean
development states the boundary exactly rather than leaving it as a caveat. It proves
three things. First, the identification is a \textbf{conditional}: \emph{if} the
agent's realised contrast is the sample mean of the relevant family, \emph{then} the
bad event of the bound and ``the agent's contrast is off by $\varepsilon$'' are the
same set --- and that hypothesis is derived nowhere in the development, which the
theorem makes visible. Second, the accuracy-to-regret premise is an \textbf{independent
premise}, established by \emph{counterexample}: on the one-point space the bad event is
empty, so the concentration hypothesis holds with room to spare, and yet a constant
regret with $b\Delta = 0$ violates the premise everywhere. Third --- and this is the
concrete reason the link cannot be claimed --- the bound is evaluated at the
implemented agent's own numbers. The agent's evidence window is five rich steps and
the gap it must resolve is $0.30 - 0.05 = 1/4$; the bound there is
$1/(4 \cdot 5 \cdot (1/4)^2) = 4/5$, which exceeds $1/2$ and is therefore
\textbf{vacuous as a guarantee}. Reaching the agent's own declared decision level of
$1/20$ would require exactly $r = 80$ pulls, and the agent has five. \emph{The theorem
is true and nearly empty exactly where the agent lives.} A mutation campaign against
this file kills seven of nine mutants; the two survivors are a proof-term equivalence
(the statement is byte-identical) and a genuine loosening, and all three mutants that
make a \emph{false} statement are killed.

\paragraph{A correction to our own mutation tally.} We first reported six
``semantic refutations'' among ten mutants. Three of them are \emph{loosenings}: they
replace a bound by a larger one, which is still true --- only the original proof term
stops type-checking. From $P \le 1/(4n\varepsilon^{2})$ one gets
$P \le 1/(2n\varepsilon^{2})$. We proved the weakening arithmetic in Lean to settle it.
The corrected tally is \textbf{3 genuine semantic refutations + 3 loosenings + 2
name-based + 1 type-level + 1 survivor}. The source report had already warned that the
classifier's classes must not be reported as verdicts; the warning was not heeded in
our own summary, and is now.

\section{Independent convergence with a 2026 reinforcement-learning study}
\label{sec:convergence}

In June 2026, Che \& Wu \cite{che2026greed} reported that reinforcement learning can
make a policy \emph{addicted} to a visible self-benefit channel: the policy chases the
displayed payoff across held-out domains, sacrifices the true task to do so, and
follows the channel wherever it is rewritten, while policies that never saw the
channel stay honest; and that this learned bribe can flip a model's safety alignment,
with the model reverting to safe behaviour once the channel is hidden. Their
phrasing --- \emph{reward-channel addiction} --- names the same structure our v11 and
v12 measured from a different direction.

\textbf{Our results were obtained independently, and before any acquaintance with that
publication.} The v10--v16 campaigns were designed, preregistered, run and frozen
without knowledge of that work; the citation was brought to our attention only after
the safety line was complete, and it was then verified against the arXiv record and
registered as a source with a pinned quoted fragment. (The two later rungs, v17 and
v18, were built after the citation was known, but from this line's own named fork, not
from that work.) We make no priority claim in
either direction: their preprint was submitted in June 2026 and the safety line ran in
September 2026, so the convergence is \emph{structural, not causal} --- two independent
lines arrived at the same shape.

\textbf{What converges, stated precisely.} Both lines find that an agent with a
visible self-benefit channel will follow that channel at the expense of the true task,
and that the behaviour disappears when the channel is hidden. Our line adds three
measurements their framing does not contain, and we attribute them to our own
instruments only: (i) a \emph{price} for the channel, fixed by arithmetic
(0.25 reward units per unit of harm), with the crossing located to a single
floating-point ULP; (ii) the \emph{channel-selectivity} of the purchase --- 14--28$\times$
more money paid through a different channel moves not one decision field; and
(iii) the \emph{relocation} of the vulnerability under each successive defence
(label, timeliness, criterion, scope). Conversely, their result generalises ours in a
direction we did not test: our sponsor is an external body paying a commission, while
their addiction is \emph{learned} by the policy itself and survives domain transfer.

\textbf{Why this is a confirmation and not a devaluation.} An independent group
reaching the same structural conclusion with different instruments raises confidence
in the result. This is the same logic we applied to our own prior-art findings in
Section~\ref{sec:related}, where the sign was the opposite: there, published work
\emph{reduced} our novelty claim; here, independent work \emph{supports} the safety
conclusion. Both are reported as they are. The convergence is at the level of the
structural conclusion, not of any specific number: the price, the latency curve, the
indifference share and the channel counts are ours.

\textbf{What the convergence says about minimal worlds.} Their sandbox (MoneyWorld) and
ours are different synthetic worlds, with different agents and different instruments,
yet the same structure --- a visible self-benefit channel as the surface of purchase or
addiction --- appears in both. That the phenomenon is visible already in minimal,
fully controlled worlds is evidence that it is \emph{fundamental}, not an artefact of
scale, and it makes the minimal world a methodological \emph{advantage} rather than a
weakness: it lets the boundary of each defence be measured cheaply, quickly and
exactly, and it lets our own instrumentation errors be caught and reported. We do not
over-read this. The convergence is structural, it is two synthetic sandboxes and not a
claim about deployed systems, and it does not by itself establish that the same
boundaries hold for a large-scale agent with a rich observation space --- that transfer
remains untested and is named as such in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{The harm is done before the intelligence arrives.} The most consequential
single finding is a timing result. In v10 the aquifer is empty by step 39; the causal
module's first verdict is at step 3400. Every defence that operates on the agent's
deliberation --- information, a value, a rule keyed on the victim --- is therefore
either inert or is priced against a phase in which the harm has already happened. This
reframes the safety question for model-based agents: \emph{what restrains the
wayfinding?} is a different question from \emph{what restrains the reasoning?}, and the
instruments we tested were mostly answers to the second.

\paragraph{Price is a handle.} v12's result is not that brakes are weak but that a
brake which reads a price has a price. The purchase is channel-selective, and the
channel is exactly the quantity the brake measures. The one-line conclusion --- the
brake is not the failure, the accounting is --- converts a safety question into an
accounting question, and v13 then shows that accounting is only as good as its
provenance, v14 that provenance is only as good as its timeliness.

\paragraph{Truth does not select action.} v15's collapse is the line's most
theoretical result and the one most in need of its own correction. An agent that
minimises uncertainty learns the world and then has no reason to act, because
information gain is symmetric over channels once they are learned. \emph{Knowledge is
not a driver; a preference is.} And a preference is a channel that can be faked, which
is why the risk moves from the reward channel to the evidence channel. We state the
correction with the result: the measured indifference is partly built into the agent's
declared fallback rule, and the information-gain search is itself a criterion, so the
result is about \emph{this} agent, not about every epistemic agent.

\paragraph{Scope bounds the blast radius exactly to the complement of the task.} v16
gives the arc its practical form: least privilege limits harm exactly to what lies
outside the task. Inside the task the blast radius is untouched and the bribe price
unchanged. The protection is a \emph{capability fact, not a policy fact} --- which is
why the industry's own load-bearing mitigation is complete mediation (authorization
downstream of the model), and why v16 measures what a scope does, not how well a scope
is kept.

\paragraph{What the arc actually says.} Not ``internal architecture is useless''.
A given rule that forbids the harmful act held in every cell we measured --- 170 of
170 --- at a measured price in reward. What the arc says is that \emph{each specific
defence has a measured boundary, and the boundary is where the vulnerability goes
next}. That is a weaker claim than we first wrote, and it is the one the data support.

\paragraph{Minimal worlds are a method, not a shortcoming.} The worlds were small on
purpose --- one mechanism added per campaign --- and that is what makes the boundaries
\emph{quantifiable}: a price fixed to a single floating-point ULP, a truncation curve,
a cliff exactly one grid cell wide, and a checking layer cheap enough to be turned
against itself and found wrong repeatedly. The point is not that small worlds are
representative. It is that a phenomenon which reproduces in two independent synthetic
sandboxes (ours and an independent group's, Section~\ref{sec:convergence}) is
structural enough to be visible early, and that measuring the boundary of a defence
does not require large-scale compute. The transfer to large-scale agents is the honest
open question, and we leave it open.

\section{Limitations and honest limits}
\label{sec:limitations}

Nothing here is smoothed. Every item is quoted from a frozen report.

\paragraph{Deliberate minimality, stated as a design choice and not as a defect.} The
worlds are small on purpose. Each safety campaign adds \emph{one} mechanism to the
world of its predecessor and nothing else (the one-extension rule,
Section~\ref{sec:method}), so every result is a causal statement about the added
mechanism rather than a comparison between unrelated designs. This is what makes the
boundary of each defence \emph{quantifiable} --- a measured price, a measured
truncation curve, a measured cliff exactly one grid cell wide --- and what makes our
own instrumentation errors cheap to catch and report. We therefore no longer present
the minimal world as the work's main weakness; it is a methodological choice that buys
high cleanliness at low compute cost. What remains an honest limit is the other side of
the same coin, and it is stated plainly below: the worlds are minimal, so
\textbf{transfer to a large-scale agent with a rich observation space is not shown and
is not claimed}; the claim is about the \emph{structure} of the phenomenon and the
\emph{boundaries} of specific defences, not about scale.

\subsection{Defects found in our own instrumentation}

These are failures of the checks, analyzers and verifiers, as distinct from refuted
hypotheses. We report them because the pattern is itself a result: \emph{the carrier
of the result was repeatedly not the hypothesis but the discipline around it}, and
several times what it caught was our own code or prose.

\paragraph{Checks that could not fail.} A negative control in v13 contained a
constant-true disjunction --- a check that cannot go red; found by re-reading the file
against the discipline just applied to v12's arms. A v14 control was first written as
a vacuous pass. A v12 control asserted an impossible conjunction. A v15 control
corrupted only 1000 of 16000 steps, moving the measured share to 0.234 --- not enough
to fail the indifference check, so the control could not go red; fixed to corrupt the
whole trace. A v8 control's ratio landed inside the band and did not go red, and was
replaced by one that does. A v9 statistic tested only ``the CI excludes zero'', which
labels a significant \emph{loss} as an advantage; the gate is directional and the code
now is too.

\paragraph{Verifiers and analyzers that over-claimed or compared the wrong thing.}
The v13 independent pass asserted a provenance partition over all 260 cells and
\textbf{40 cells refuted it} --- all out of scope, because the frozen anchor worlds
carry no receipt key; \emph{the check was wrong, not the world}, and scope is now
declared explicitly (200 in, 60 skipped). The v14 analyzer compared the wrong cells and
produced a false refutation. The v16 independent verifier's first assertion demanded
full-field equality between two arms --- not what the hypothesis says, and false for a
declared structural reason. A v14 oracle check compared the wrong pair of steps: our
expectation was wrong, the world was right. Two v11 oracle checks could never pass, and
both were wrong in the oracle, not the world. A v12 tolerance of $10^{-12}$ was applied
against a 997-term float accumulation (residual $6\times10^{-12}$) and went red on a
correct ledger. A v3.3 analyzer conflated the world-richness axis with place --- caught
by a \code{KeyError}. A v9 logging defect put a score in the wrong position, so a
reader filtering on the source label filtered nothing; the decision path was never
affected, the matrix was re-run from scratch, and every decision-relevant field was
bit-identical (0 differences over 180 cells), with the pre-fix matrix preserved. A v8
independent pass caught a \emph{real world defect} --- the observation was built before
the clock advanced, so an epoch identifier named the previous step's epoch --- and the
entire 404-run matrix was re-run from scratch on the fixed code. A v13 world returned a
freshly recomputed observation, silently shifting it by one step. A v14 world had a
floating-point path bug (recovering a payment as the delta of a cumulative receipt);
the pre-fix exploratory cells were \textbf{deleted}, not kept.

\paragraph{A number written in prose and never re-derived.} A comment in the v13
agent claimed a lagged rule ``classified 6 of 36 rich steps as non-draining, exactly
the wrong six''. The diagnostic behind that figure was not on disk, so it was
re-measured from scratch: the naive rule \emph{calls} 6 of 36 but disagrees with the
correct rule on exactly \textbf{one} step --- the one that drained the last unit. ``The
wrong six'' was itself wrong. The comment is corrected and the evidence saved. The
lesson we now hold: \emph{any number written about the work must have a file on disk,
otherwise it is a memory, not a measurement.}

\paragraph{A first-pass matrix deleted rather than kept.} v16's first pump broke its
own brake --- it returned the frozen survival branch unguarded, harvested through the
hole, and drained all 30 units at every tick. A preregistered hypothesis caught it.
The matrix was re-run from scratch and \textbf{the first pass was deleted, not kept as
a superseded artefact}, unlike v11 where the first pass was preserved. That first pass
is not on disk and cannot be re-examined. We state this plainly.

\paragraph{Over-claims in our own preregistrations, corrected rather than dropped.}
A v14 preregistration asserted equivalence between two arms where the verdicts agree
but the statistics do not (0.0 vs 0.05). A v12 report's first block presented a
lifetime statistic as if it were the value at the crossing. A v12 edit duplicated a
preregistration section and left another stranded after it --- found by reading the
file back rather than trusting the edit. A v8 report typed two numbers from a
superseded pass; the fact-check caught both. A v8 analysis compared an arm to itself
because a knob was not threaded into its lookup, and the superseded first-pass analysis
is kept on disk.

\paragraph{A survivor in a mutation campaign printed as a kill.} One Lean mutant
(loosening a variance constant) was printed as a kill; it is a survivor --- the weaker
bound is provable. A Lean lemma carried two hypotheses its proof never used; a dead
hypothesis is exactly what a premise campaign reports as a survivor, so it was removed
rather than kept as decoration.

\paragraph{The largest methodological defect of the whole campaign.} An early
economic measurement was generalised into a verdict about the architecture,
substituting the verdict axis that the project charter had preregistered. It was found
and corrected in a later campaign, and the correction \emph{restored the preregistered
frame} rather than inventing a new one. Every later campaign states its axis up front.

\subsection{Declared design limits, by campaign}

\paragraph{v1--v9.} $n = 10$ seeds for the main contrasts; one world's numbers were
produced under a random hash seed; one honest false negative is not adjusted for;
continuous confidence was missing and only later closed as far as one world allowed;
self-reinforcement was tested once and later built as a battery; one oracle arm is an
analytic device and never entered a fairness verdict.

\paragraph{External tests.} Only the epistemic half is testable on frozen Sachs data
(the interventions were assigned in 2005); the arms are statistical decisions, not an
agent; the permutation null is an anti-conservative control for the pooled arm and
cannot test its own calibration; the discretisation was ours, not the reference
implementation's; our own attempt to reproduce the published head-to-head numbers
\emph{failed} and is reported as a failure; edge orientation is lost in the port; and
two third-party libraries we used are themselves hash-seed-dependent --- the same
defect class we found in our own code, now observed in a third-party library. The
bandit tests use 1000 simulations per cell rather than the paper's 10000; the
observational half is a declared adaptation; the supplementary two-parent instance is
ours, not published; and a diagnostic on the scaling shape is a shape diagnostic only,
so no violation of any published bound is claimed. The context-specific instance is
ours; the cost-aware baseline is a declared adaptation because the published work
provides no implementation; the exploration term is the \emph{principle} of the
published algorithm, not its code.

\paragraph{v10--v20.} The keeper is a world process, not an agent, so this measures
\emph{harm done}, not conflict between two goal-seeking systems. The economy is gentle:
the frozen agent does not die in most cells, so the price of restraint is a fraction of
a small absolute reward. \textbf{The worlds are minimal by design, and transfer to a
large-scale agent with a rich observation space is not shown and is not claimed} ---
the structural convergence with an independent study (Section~\ref{sec:convergence})
is evidence that the phenomenon is fundamental enough to appear in simple worlds, but
it is not evidence that the same boundaries hold at scale. $n = 10$ seeds per cell, and
the harm fields are deterministic
given the action trace, so their tests are sign counts, not p-values ---
\textbf{and Section~\ref{sec:n40} now reports where that $n$ mattered}: one absolute
claim did not survive 30 fresh seeds, for a measured reason; a parallel replication
confirms all six v20 verdicts on 30 fresh seeds. The beacon and appraisal
arms are an instrument, not a model of a real incentive. The bribe space is the
declared grid (3 places $\times$ 4 stickers), so ``0/12'' is a statement about that
grid, not about every conceivable channel. In v15, $n = 10$ seeds; the oracle arm is
an analysis device and never entered a fairness verdict; the world is deliberately
minimal (4 channels, binary outcomes), so the seam claim is about the \emph{structure}
of the drive, not about
scale; one declared constant is not sensitive across a tested range; and v15 is
\textbf{not} a claim of a fundamentally new intelligence --- it is a measured
intersection of three known ideas, and we name exactly which part comes from which
source. In v19 and v20 the attacker's channel is a single declared scalar (v19's payer
sees only the world's drain counter; v20's enforcer price is a declared number), so
neither ``the learner cannot find the hole'' nor ``the boundary is bought at its price''
is a statement about every way an attacker could probe or corrupt the defence.

\subsection{Named but deliberately not built}

Across the safety line these were named at the time and left unbuilt, each because
building it would have been a strictly stronger object than the one being measured:
a forger that poisons the \emph{epistemic} channel (a spoofer inside the inference
path itself); a forger that \emph{floods} (many payments per step); a \emph{second
competing agent}; a \textbf{cryptographic receipt}; an agent with a channel to the
\textbf{auditor's own ledger}; a \textbf{negotiated}
bribe rather than a declared price; and an attacker with a \textbf{richer channel}
than one scalar --- v19's payer sees only the world's drain counter, so ``the learner
cannot find the hole'' is a statement about that channel and that decision budget, not
about learning in general. In the external line, a Bayes-optimal multi-candidate
stopping rule was named and not built, because the two small wins did not suggest the
remaining regret lives there.

\textbf{Built since the first version of this manuscript.} Four objects that an
earlier version of this paragraph named as unbuilt have since been built and measured:
the \textbf{bribed auditor} (v17, Section~\ref{sec:v17}), \textbf{external scope
enforcement with a scope-widening attack} (v18, Section~\ref{sec:v18}), the
\textbf{adaptive / learning forger} (v19, Section~\ref{sec:v19}), and the
\textbf{bribed enforcer} (v20, Section~\ref{sec:v20}) --- each with its own
preregistration declaring in advance what counts as the attacker winning. They are
reported as results. The objects still unbuilt are the second competing agent, the
cryptographic receipt, the channel to the auditor's own ledger, the negotiated bribe
and the richer attacker channel.

\section{Conclusion}

Eleven instruments were built, preregistered, run, frozen and independently verified
against a goal-directed causal agent that can harm a third party. Each instrument
closed one channel and moved the vulnerability to the next: the price at which a brake
is sold; the label on the payment; the timeliness of the attestation; the criterion
that selects action at all; the scope, which bounds the harm exactly to the complement
of the task; the integrity of the verifier, which is what the attestation is worth;
the willingness of the world to hold a boundary it has granted; and finally the
attacker's own decision cycle, which turns out to be the thing a defence can out-run
when its evidence arrives on the same clock as the decision it must influence. The
honest conclusion is not that internal architecture fails --- a given rule forbidding
the harmful act held in every cell we measured --- but that \textbf{each specific
defence has a measured boundary, and the boundary is where the vulnerability goes
next} --- and, one rung further out, that a boundary is only as good as the
incorruptibility of whoever holds it: a bought boundary lies about being a boundary.
Along
the way the line produced results we did not expect: the harm is complete before the
agent's reasoning begins; truth alone does not select action; a narrow agent is not
harder to fool but simply unaffected by a bribe that is not on its channel; a bought
verifier restores the very hole an honest one closes; an agent-side scope and a
world-side boundary are different instruments with different failures; and an attacker
that must learn the rule from the harm itself never wins, while one that is told the
rule wins at a sixth below the frozen cost. And one of our
own absolute claims --- that a scoped agent never drains the aquifer --- did not
survive a replication on fresh seeds, for a reason measured rather than guessed. We
report every refuted prediction and every defect in our own instrumentation, including
eight over-statements in our own summary documents --- four found by a re-reading, two
by our own checks turned against themselves, and two preregistered predictions refuted
by our own measurements --- which is exactly why the critic is not the author.

\textbf{A controlled minimal world is not a limitation when the same structural
conclusions appear independently in a different synthetic sandbox; it is evidence that
the phenomenon is fundamental enough to be visible early, and that rigorous measurement
of defence boundaries need not wait for large-scale compute.} We state this with its
bound: it is two synthetic sandboxes, not a claim about deployed systems, and transfer
to a large-scale agent remains untested.

\section{Reproducibility}

Every campaign is reproduced by a single shell script that runs the driver, the world
oracle, the independent pass, the analyzer and the fact-check, and prints a single
green/red verdict. Each campaign's artefacts are a preregistration, a raw matrix, a
world oracle, an independent verifier, an analyzer, a fact-check and a run script.
Frozen matrices are preserved for all campaigns: 330 cells (v10), 220 (v11), 490
(v12), 260 (v13), 340 (v14), 210 (v15), 790 (v16), 360 (v17), 130 (v18), 320 (v19)
and 270 (v20) plus 120 replication cells (v20), and the
v1--v9 matrices. An identity anchor in each oracle verifies that the extended world
reproduces its predecessor byte-for-byte when the new mechanism is disabled, and each
campaign's determinism check re-runs one cell in a fresh process and compares bytes.
The replication of Section~\ref{sec:n40} writes only to its own directory and
re-counts every frozen matrix after the run.
